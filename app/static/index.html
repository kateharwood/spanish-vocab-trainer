How studying works

Where the words come from

Every word comes from a list of the ~5,000 most frequent Spanish words, ranked by how often each one appears. The # next to a word is its rank, so #12 is the 12th most common word.

Variants of one word (negro / negra / negros, or hablar / hablando / hablado) are grouped into a family. A family is one card: you see every English meaning, recall the Spanish, and the rating applies to all of its forms.

Only words you sorted into Levels 1–4 or Verb practice are studied. Level 0, Bad entries, Don't care, and unsorted words never appear.

How repetition is scheduled

Reviews are scheduled with FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler). For each card it tracks how hard the word is for you and how long you're likely to remember it, then schedules the next review for when your odds of remembering it drop to about 90%. Each successful review pushes the next one further out; forgetting brings it back sooner.

Under each rating button is when you'd see the card again: a number of days, or repeat, which means it comes back later in this same session, after the cards still ahead of it. Repeats never make you wait; when they're all that's left, they come right up.

What the progress numbers mean

Every word you've started is in exactly one of these stages:

  • Learning — you've just met or just forgotten the word and it's on a short repeat (minutes) inside the current session. This is normally 0 between sessions, since a session ends once its repeats are done.
  • Young — in regular review, but FSRS isn't yet confident you'll remember it three weeks from now.
  • Mature — FSRS estimates you'll still have about a 90% chance of remembering it after 21 days or more without review (its "stability"). Words get there by being recalled correctly across growing gaps; recalling a word after a long break gets it there fastest.

Due now, due in 24 hours, and due this week count started words by when they're next scheduled, whatever their stage.
